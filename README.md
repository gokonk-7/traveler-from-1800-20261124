One sentence, 144 characters, that explains the modern internet to a time traveler who has just stepped out of the year 1800 — using no word coined after their time, no list of features, just one picture they could hold in their head:

> Imagine a sea of ink in which every mind on earth dips its pen at once, so that a word written in London is read in Canton before the ink dries.

The angle is a sea of ink, not a library: the internet is an act everyone does at once — writing and reading, across the world, before the ink dries — not a building you visit. London to Canton names the longest trade route a traveler from 1800 knows, so the sentence says "the whole world, instantly" without ever saying "world" or "instantly". Every content word was audited against its first attestation (see `NOTES.md`); the rejected variants and the reasoning are documented there too.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Prints exactly the sentence in `sentence.txt` and nothing else. Tests:

```sh
python3 test_sentence.py
```

`test_sentence.py` checks the brief's hard rules: one sentence, at most 200 characters, no post-1800 words, no feature list, scale + two-way writing and reading + global reach + instantaneity, and deterministic output.
